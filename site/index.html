Free and open source for Scalable Capital investors

When will my portfolio reach my goal?

Zielzeit keeps the answer in your menu bar. It reads your account through the official Scalable CLI, read‑only, with no scraping and no credentials, then turns your balance, your savings plan and the return you actually earned into one year.

Features

A year you can see without opening anything

Three scenarios on one chart

Cautious at 3%, moderate at 6%, and your own pace: the return your portfolio actually achieved over the trailing year, measured with the simple Dietz method against your real deposits.

Each curve stops where it meets the goal, so the comparison is a picture rather than a table. The headline uses your pace, not a house assumption, so the year moves with how you are actually doing.

Two what-if sliders, both ways round

Save more previews what another €200 a month does to every projection, live, without changing anything in your broker.

Reach by inverts the question: pick a year and it tells you the monthly contribution that lands on it. If growth alone gets you there, it says so in words rather than printing a zero.

Measured, not guessed

Your contributions are walked from a year of real transactions and summed as deposits less withdrawals, rather than inferred from today's savings plan rate. On a live account that difference was over a full percentage point.

Inflation is on screen too: the goal restated in today's money at the projected horizon, shown plainly rather than hidden behind a toggle.

English and German

Picked from your Mac's language and switchable in the app. Amounts follow the language, so it is €250 000 in English and 250 000 € in German, with the symbol on the side each convention puts it.

Safety

Read-only, and checkable in a few minutes

Zielzeit can run exactly five commands against your broker. There is no route to a trade, to any other write command, or to login. Every claim below names the file that backs it, so you can check rather than take it on faith.

It cannot trade, sell, or move money

Five commands are enumerated in one Swift type, and every call site passes one of them. No shell is involved: the CLI runs through Process directly, not a string that could be made to mean something else.

grep -A20 'enum Command' \
  Sources/ZielzeitCore/ScalableClient.swift

It never sees your password or your 2FA

It never runs sc login. Signing in is an OAuth device-code flow you complete yourself; the app shows you the command and can open Terminal with it typed but not executed.

sc login --local-read-only
# you run this, never the app

Your data cannot leave your Mac

There is no networking code in the app at all: no URLSession, no sockets, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporter, no account. The only outbound traffic is the official CLI talking to your broker.

grep -rn 'URLSession' Sources/
# returns nothing

It keeps three values, and no figures

Your goal amount, your language, and whether you have emailed for beta access. No balance, no holdings, no transactions, no name. Those are fetched, shown, and forgotten when the app quits.

defaults read com.zielzeit.Zielzeit

macOS will warn you once

The download is signed but not notarized, and notarization needs a paid Apple Developer account this project does not have. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway. Once, ever. Or build from source, and a local build is never flagged.

It is not sandboxed, and cannot be

A sandboxed app cannot launch another program, and Zielzeit's whole job is to run the CLI you installed. So it runs with your normal user permissions. What limits it is that it is small, open, and readable end to end, not an OS boundary.

Setup

Three steps, one of them an email

The Scalable CLI is in beta and gated: Scalable Capital has to allowlist your machine before it can log in at all. That is a human round-trip nobody can automate away, so Zielzeit makes every step around it one tap. The app walks you through all three on first launch.

  1. Install the CLI

    From Scalable Capital's own Homebrew tap. Zielzeit never bundles the binary, because their guidance is to trust only official artifacts.

    brew install scalable-cli

  2. Request beta access

    Zielzeit reads your installation code and opens a prefilled email to cli.beta@scalable.capital. Nothing to compose.

    Send it from the address registered with Scalable Capital. They match the request to your account by sender, and a request from any other address is silently never answered. A mailto: link opens your default mail account, which often is not that one, so check the From field.

  3. Sign in yourself

    An OAuth device-code flow, in your Terminal and your browser. The --local-read-only flag stores the session in locally enforced read-only mode, which makes the promise structural rather than editorial.

    sc login --local-read-only

Or build it yourself

About a minute, and no Gatekeeper prompt.

git clone https://github.com/Mannafee/zielzeit-scalable-capital.git
cd zielzeit-scalable-capital
make run

How it works

Two formulas, and a test that keeps them honest

Your realized return, simple Dietz

rate = gain₁ᵧ ÷ (total − gain₁ᵧ − contributions ÷ 2)

Contributions are measured, not guessed. Securities movements are excluded, because a custody migration would otherwise look like a year of deposits. The rate is clamped to ±30%, and suppressed entirely when the capital base works out to zero or less. That is what happens to a portfolio younger than a year, where deposits rather than growth explain the balance.

Time to the goal

t = ln((G·r + P) ÷ (V·r + P)) ÷ ln(1 + r)

G goal, V current value, P monthly contribution, r monthly rate converted geometrically. If your savings plan steps up each year, the contribution becomes a step function the closed form cannot express, so Zielzeit walks forward one twelve-month block at a time and applies the same formula at each year's contribution.

The chart plots that same recurrence forward, and a test asserts each curve meets the goal line at exactly the month the formula returns. That cross-check is what stops the picture and the headline from quietly disagreeing.

These are projections, not predictions. They assume a constant return, smooth compounding and an uninterrupted savings plan, none of which is how markets or life work. Before tax. Not financial advice.
